The What-If Lab

What this page is: a reading guide. Twenty-four real scientific mysteries, taken from two science videos, are each paired with novels that ask "if this were true, what would it do to us?" Every pairing is graded for how closely the book uses the actual science, so you can tell a direct hit from a loose echo.

Fact-check notes

Where the original notes were loose, this page tightens them. Awards listed are only the ones confirmed; a dash means none to report.

About dates, page counts and editions

"First published" is the first appearance of the book, or of the original-language edition. Page counts are rounded to the nearest 50 and vary by edition, format and font, so treat them as a guide to length, not an exact figure.

Why only three "direct hits"?

Very few novels use the exact phenomenon. Hominids, Mexican Gothic and The Postmortal come closest. Others were moved to "close extrapolation" or "thematic echo".

The Echo Maker is not a fictional Awakenings

Awakenings is Oliver Sacks' nonfiction book. Richard Powers' novel is about Capgras syndrome after a brain injury, so it is matched on neurological identity, not on encephalitis lethargica.

Semiosis and the slime mold

The intelligent organism in Semiosis is a plant, not a mold. It fits "intelligence without a brain", not slime molds specifically.

Blood Music and HeLa cells

Blood Music imagines cells that become intelligent. HeLa cells are immortal, not intelligent, so the link is by theme.

Contact, House of Suns and Seveneves

Contact concerns a deliberate signal, not the unexplained cosmic radio background. House of Suns is not about the Boötes Void specifically. Seveneves imagines a lunar catastrophe, not a Miyake radiation burst.
