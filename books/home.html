Ultimate Reading Universe

121 mind-bending books in a deliberate order, each one placed to prepare you for the next.

  • 121 books
  • 86 sequence + 35 companions
  • difficulty 1–10

What you can do

  • Read in order. Start at #1, Flowers for Algernon. A “Why here?” note explains every placement.
  • Find your level. Every book has a 1–10 difficulty rating, so you can pick an entry point.
  • Filter and search. By genre, reading status, bought or will-buy. Search by title or author, or press / anywhere on the page.
  • Track your reading. Mark books Reading or Finished, keep personal notes and watch the progress bar. Sign in to sync across devices, or export your progress as JSON.
  • Choose a look. Light, dark or a deep-ocean theme with ambient sound. Tap a cover to enlarge it, or switch to Names Only for a compact list.

What's inside

The sequence (86). Opens with accessible modern classics like Flowers for Algernon, At the Mountains of Madness and Piranesi, builds through Cryptonomicon, Permutation City and Anathem, and ends on the hardest reads: Dhalgren, House of Leaves, The Sound and the Fury and Infinite Jest.

The companions (35). Side reads that pair with the sequence, including Beckett's Molloy trilogy, Kafka's The Trial and The Castle, The Dispossessed, Hyperion and Roadside Picnic.

Two Paths Through Uncertainty

36 books for a statistical mind: 18 novels where mathematics drives the plot, and 18 nonfiction books that build the toolkit for reasoning under uncertainty.

  • 36 books
  • 18 fiction + 18 nonfiction
  • Essential Six starter shelf

What you can do

  • Walk two paths. A five-level statistical path of nonfiction, and a nine-novel narrative path. Tap any stage to filter the catalog to those books.
  • Start small. Tap Essential Six for a six-book starter shelf, or hit Surprise me.
  • Search, filter and sort. Fiction, nonfiction or topic chips. Sort by reading order, highest rated, easiest first or A–Z.
  • Read before you buy. Every book has a spoiler-free description, a star rating and a one-line reason it earns its place. Nonfiction adds a difficulty level.
  • Tick off what you've explored. A “0 / 36 explored” counter keeps your place, saved in your browser.

What's inside

Fiction (18). Novels where a mathematician or statistician is a character, or where mathematical thinking drives the plot: The Oxford Murders, Uncle Petros and Goldbach's Conjecture, The Housekeeper and the Professor, Contact and Permutation City.

Nonfiction (18). The toolkit itself: The Drunkard's Walk, The Signal and the Noise, Thinking, Fast and Slow, The Book of Why, Fooled by Randomness and Jaynes's Probability Theory.

The What-If Lab

31 novels for 24 unsolved scientific mysteries: pick a stained cell under the microscope and get the books that ask what happens if it is true.

  • 31 novels
  • 24 mysteries
  • graded by directness

What you can do

  • Look through the lens. Twenty-four stained cells, one per unsolved mystery. Tap one to filter the novels.
  • Read the science. Each mystery has a short plain explanation and the “what if” a novelist would start from.
  • Check the grade. Every pairing is marked direct hit, close extrapolation or thematic echo.
  • Search, sort and pull a slide. By mystery, year, length or title, or pull a random novel. Every slide lists publisher, page count and more.
  • Track and talk. A reading log saved in your browser, a timeline from 1864 to 2022, and reader notes.

What's inside

Direct hits (4). Blindsight, Hominids, Mexican Gothic and The Postmortal use the exact science.

The rest (27). Close extrapolations and thematic echoes, from Children of Time and Anathem to Seveneves and Dragon's Egg, plus fact-check notes on where the source pairings were loose.

  • Neuroscience
  • Evolution
  • Ageing
  • Cosmology
  • Deep Earth
  • Fungi and cells
